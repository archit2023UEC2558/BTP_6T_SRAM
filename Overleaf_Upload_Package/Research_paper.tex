\begin{abstract}
Embedded static random-access memory (SRAM) occupies upwards of 70\% of modern system-on-chip (SoC) die area, making parametric fault diagnosis critical to semiconductor yield and reliability. In scaled CMOS technologies, subtle physical defects, including access-contact resistive opens, weak dielectric bridging, and aging-induced threshold voltage ($V_{\text{th}}$) drift, degrade stability and timing margins, yet 93.4\% of resistive-open and $V_{\text{th}}$-drift instances (77.3\% across all defect classes) pass all four operational screening checks in simulation, indicating that they may evade basic functional screening. Although machine learning (ML) classifiers accurately diagnose these faults under nominal conditions, environmental shifts across Process, Voltage, and Temperature (PVT) corners induce severe out-of-distribution collapse on raw features ($50.00\%$ worst-corner floor), driven by pervasive inter-defect confusion between contact resistance and channel overdrive loss.

To eliminate post-deployment recalibration across environmental corners without on-chip temperature-sensor telemetry, this paper proposes a Physics-Normalized Invariant Feature framework that references target cell metrics against a matched concurrent on-die reference cell ($X_{\text{inv}} = X_{\text{meas}} / X_{\text{ref}}$, assuming matched devices) to suppress common-mode environmental variation. Validated across 1,350 SPICE instances spanning a 45-corner PVT grid (TT/FF/SS, $0.9\text{--}1.1\,\text{V}$, $-40^\circ\text{C}$ to $125^\circ\text{C}$) on a low-voltage $1.0\,\text{V}$ $180\,\text{nm}$ CMOS benchmark, the invariant formulation elevates 45-fold Leave-One-Corner-Out (LOCO) mean accuracy from $75.11\%$ to $99.33\%$ (Random Forest) and raises the worst-corner floor from $50.00\%$ to $90.00\%$. Furthermore, under Leave-One-Severity-Out (LOSO) interpolation across intermediate severities ($S_2$–$S_5$, $99.54\%$, with $90.74\%$ on incipient $S_1$ defects) and a joint holdout simultaneously withholding corners and intermediate severities ($99.11\%$), the framework demonstrates robust dual-axis generalization. Tree SHAP attributions are consistent with access-path impedance and switching physics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The relentless scaling of complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) technologies into deep sub-micron nodes has propelled embedded static random-access memory (SRAM) to constitute 70\% to 80\% of modern System-on-Chip (SoC) dies \cite{mukhopadhyay2005modeling}. Consequently, memory yield, defect resilience, and post-silicon diagnostic fidelity represent important determinants of total wafer yield, cost, and field longevity in mission-critical applications spanning aerospace, automotive electronics, and ultra-low-power biomedical devices.

In scaled and deep sub-micron technologies, parametric defects have become increasingly important yield- and reliability-limiting mechanisms in addition to hard, catastrophic defects \cite{hamdioui2003dynamic}. These include incomplete contact/via etch (resistive opens), thin-oxide inter-layer leakage slivers (weak bridging faults), and threshold voltage shifts induced by Negative/Positive Bias Temperature Instability (NBTI/PBTI) and Hot-Carrier Injection (HCI) aging ($V_{\text{th}}$ shifts). Crucially, empirical simulation reveals that 93.43\% of resistive-open and $V_{\text{th}}$-drift defect samples (and 77.32\% across all defect classes) retain functional bistability and pass all four operational screening checks (Hold stability, Write completion, Read disturbance, and Bistability) at the test condition, indicating that such defects may evade conventional functional screening. In contrast, internal bridging shorts continuously conduct DC quiescent current across the cross-coupled storage nodes, causing bridging instances to fail at least one operational screening check (with low-resistance bridges $R_{\text{bridge}} \le 1\,\text{k}\Omega$ additionally collapsing regenerative feedback and destroying bistability). For the vast majority of non-catastrophic parametric flaws, cells switch logically under nominal conditions while exhibiting severely compromised Static Noise Margins (SNM), latent dynamic write failures under supply droop, and elevated standby leakage current ($I_{\text{DDQ}}$).

To detect and categorize these latent flaws before catastrophic deployment failure, analog built-in current testing ($I_{\text{DDQ}}$) and machine-learning-assisted diagnosis have recently gained substantial traction. Supervised classifiers---such as Random Forests (RF), Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Gradient Boosted Decision Trees---can map high-dimensional analog signatures (timing delays, quiescent currents, bitline sense swings) into multi-class defect categories with near-perfect accuracy under nominal testing conditions ($27^\circ\text{C}, 1.0\,\text{V}$) \cite{makris2018special, amrouch2022machine}.

\textbf{The Cross-PVT Domain Shift Failure:} Despite nominal success, classical ML approaches encounter an existential operational barrier when deployed across the real-world Process, Voltage, and Temperature (PVT) operating envelope ($-40^\circ\text{C}$ to $+125^\circ\text{C}$, $\pm 10\%$ $V_{DD}$, TT/FF/SS corners). In the representative 180\,nm LEVEL=1 SPICE benchmark, simulated total standby current increases by $119.5\times$, from $4.2\,\text{nA}$ at $27^\circ\text{C}$ to $502\,\text{nA}$ at $125^\circ\text{C}$ under nominal TT and $1.0\,\text{V}$ conditions, capturing substantial temperature sensitivity of the modeled standby-current signature. Although subthreshold conduction exhibits exponential temperature dependence in physical nanoscale MOS devices ($I_{\text{sub}} \propto \mu(T) T^2 \exp(-qV_{\text{th}}/(mkT))$), the contributions of individual leakage mechanisms in the present LEVEL=1 simulation depend on the compact-model formulation, junction parameters, and numerical conductances, serving as a controlled operational surrogate for large common-mode thermal drift. Concurrently, reducing supply voltage from $1.0\,\text{V}$ to $0.9\,\text{V}$ degrades gate overdrive ($V_{GS} - V_{\text{th}}$), expanding healthy dynamic write delay ($t_{\text{write}}$) by $19.3\%$ (and by $36.0\%$ across the full $1.1\,\text{V}$ to $0.9\,\text{V}$ supply range at TT $27^\circ\text{C}$), while the SS process corner shifts healthy peak write current by $22.9\%$ relative to TT (from $0.035\,\text{mA}$ to $0.027\,\text{mA}$). When an ML model trained on raw absolute magnitudes encounters unseen operating corners, it misinterprets environmental physical drift as structural defects, triggering severe \textbf{inter-defect confusion}. Specifically, at the reduced-voltage cold corners (SS and TT, $0.9\,\text{V}, -40^\circ\text{C}$), the higher SS nominal threshold-voltage model parameter ($V_{\text{TO},n} = 0.495\,\text{V}$), together with temperature-dependent threshold behavior ($\phi_F(T)$ drift) and reduced supply headroom, decreases effective gate overdrive and expands write delay to its global maximum ($87.6\,\text{ps}$ in SS), which mimics access contact degradation. Consequently, while other $0.9\,\text{V}$ corners retain $53.33\%$ to $86.67\%$ accuracy, the raw classifier encounters maximum inter-defect confusion at these two cold floor corners: \textbf{100\% of access pass-gate resistive opens (12 of 12) are misclassified as access-transistor $V_{\text{th}}$ aging}, while 2 storage $V_{\text{th}}$ drift samples and the 1 healthy evaluation cell are likewise misclassified as access $V_{\text{th}}$ drift (accounting for all 15 misclassifications out of 30 evaluation samples). While bridging faults are reliably separated by functional failure flags and elevated quiescent currents (drawing up to $66.94\,\mu\text{A}$ with a median of $5.49\,\mu\text{A}$, largely exceeding co-located healthy standby currents), raw decision boundaries between resistive opens, access aging, and storage aging become severely confounded. Because each held-out corner contains 29 faulty cells and only 1 healthy cell, this pervasive inter-fault confounding drops raw classifier cross-corner accuracy to a $50.00\%$ floor at both the SS and TT $0.9\,\text{V}, -40^\circ\text{C}$ corners (15 errors out of 30 samples).

To solve this without requiring per-corner recalibration or on-chip temperature-sensor telemetry at deployment (with nominal supply voltage $V_{DD}$ established from system rails), this paper introduces a \textbf{Physics-Normalized Invariant Feature} methodology. By referencing the measurements of the target cell against a matched, concurrent on-die reference cell simulated under the identical instantaneous PVT state ($X_{\text{inv}} = X_{\text{meas}} / X_{\text{ref}}$, assuming matched devices; sensitivity to reference-measurement error, but not to device mismatch, is evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:tolerance}), common-mode environmental variance is suppressed. Exact cancellation occurs only when target and reference measurements share an identical multiplicative environmental factor; otherwise, the ratio provides approximate common-mode suppression. To isolate and quantify first-order common-mode suppression without confounding higher-order layout parasitics or complex empirical fitting parameters, we evaluate this framework on a low-voltage ($1.0\,\text{V}$) $180\,\text{nm}$ CMOS cell configured with a compressed supply-to-threshold ratio ($V_{DD,\text{nom}}/|V_{\text{TO}}| \approx 2.22$). We target this diagnostic methodology specifically at post-silicon characterization test vehicles, dedicated scribe-line test structures, and yield-learning macros equipped with analog monitor pass-gates, where high-precision parametric signatures can be directly characterized to accelerate semiconductor process debugging. The primary contributions of this paper are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Automated Multi-Operation SPICE Simulation Harness:} An end-to-end Python-driven simulation and netlist mutation framework that injects four continuous parametric defect classes into a 6T SRAM cell and automates Hold, Write, Read, and DC characterizations across a comprehensive 45-corner PVT grid.
    \item \textbf{Numerical SNM Butterfly Extraction:} A direct numerical inscribed-square search performed on unrotated voltage transfer characteristics to estimate Hold and Read Static Noise Margins. The method avoids reliance on simplified peak-separation approximations and permits explicit evaluation of asymmetric or distorted butterfly lobes.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Normalized Invariant Feature Formulation:} Dimensionless, reference-normalized feature ratios and electrical invariants that suppress common-mode environmental dispersion across wide temperature and supply ranges while preserving sharp parametric defect separability.
    \item \textbf{Cross-PVT and Multi-Axis Generalization Benchmarking:} A 45-fold Leave-One-Corner-Out (LOCO) evaluation protocol assessing classification performance on withheld combinations of process, voltage, and temperature within the simulated operating grid, achieving $99.33\%$ accuracy. Additional severity-based evaluations demonstrate interpolation across intermediate defect levels ($99.54\%$ on $S_2$–$S_5$, $90.74\%$ on $S_1$) and joint generalization to simultaneously withheld PVT conditions and defect severities ($99.11\%$) without post-deployment recalibration.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Consistent Model Interpretation via Tree SHAP:} Global and class-disaggregated Shapley attribution analysis showing that learned machine learning decision boundaries are consistent with access-path impedance and latch regeneration dynamics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work \& Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Traditional SRAM Fault Testing}
Conventional SRAM testing commonly employs March algorithms, including March C- and March SS, executed via digital Built-In Self-Test (BIST) engines to detect functional faults such as stuck-at, transition, coupling, and dynamic faults \cite{vandegoor1993march, vandegoor2000functional, hamdioui2003dynamic}. Although these algorithms provide effective coverage for their targeted functional fault models, certain parametric defects may remain undetected when the resulting degradation does not violate the logic or timing requirements of the applied test sequence. For example, moderate resistive opens or transistor threshold-voltage shifts may reduce writeability, read stability, or timing margins while preserving successful digital read and write operations under nominal conditions. Specialized Design-for-Testability (DFT) schemes, such as the programmable weak-cell detection threshold technique developed by Pavlov, Sachdev, and Pineda de Gyvez \cite{pavlov2004sram}, have been proposed to detect margin-compromised cells. Nonetheless, comprehensive multi-class physical diagnosis and process yield learning benefit from high-resolution analog characterization to complement conventional March testing by revealing degradation not observable through the selected functional test conditions.

Quiescent supply-current testing ($I_{\text{DDQ}}$) has been employed to identify abnormal leakage paths and resistive bridging defects in CMOS circuits \cite{pavlov2004sram}. However, as memory-array size and background leakage increase with dimensional scaling, the incremental current contribution of an individual defective cell becomes difficult to distinguish from the aggregate leakage of millions of parallel healthy cells, drastically reducing the defect-to-noise ratio at elevated temperatures. This challenge motivates localized analog characterization and reference-based measurement techniques for detecting subtle parametric defects.

\subsection{Static Noise Margin Extraction Methodologies}
The foundational standard for SRAM stability analysis was formulated by Seevinck, List, and Lohstroh \cite{seevinck1987static}. Static Noise Margin (SNM) quantifies the maximum tolerable DC noise disturbance before a bistable SRAM cell loses its stored state. In the butterfly-curve method, the Voltage Transfer Characteristics (VTCs) of the two cross-coupled inverters are superimposed to form two closed lobes. The SNM is obtained from the smaller of the maximum square side lengths that can be inscribed within these lobes, with square edges oriented parallel to the original voltage coordinate axes:
\begin{equation}
\text{SNM} = \min(s_1, s_2)
\label{eq:snm_def}
\end{equation}
where $s_1$ and $s_2$ denote the maximum inscribed-square side lengths for the upper-left and lower-right butterfly lobes, respectively.

Crucially, cell stability must be distinguished across operational regimes. In \textbf{Hold mode} ($WL=0\,\text{V}$), access pass-gates are cut off, and Hold SNM (HSNM) characterizes the intrinsic retention stability of the isolated cross-coupled inverters. In \textbf{Read mode} ($WL=V_{DD}, BL=\overline{BL}=V_{DD}$), conducting access transistors couple the precharged bitlines to the internal storage nodes, pulling up the node storing logic ``0'' by a disturb bump voltage ($V_{\text{bump}}$) and shifting the inverter transfer characteristics inward. This compresses the enclosed butterfly lobes, yielding Read SNM ($\text{RSNM} < \text{HSNM}$) as the critical indicator of read disturbance vulnerability.

Although the $45^\circ$ coordinate transformation is mathematically exact ($u = (V_Q - V_{Qb})/\sqrt{2}$, $v = (V_Q + V_{Qb})/\sqrt{2}$), simplified extraction methods that estimate SNM from selected curve separations may introduce geometric approximations. Such approximations require care when the butterfly lobes are strongly distorted or asymmetric. The proposed method instead searches directly for the largest admissible axis-aligned square in each lobe of the original voltage-transfer characteristics.

\subsection{Machine Learning in VLSI Diagnosis and Domain Shift}
Recent research has deployed machine learning classifiers to predict SRAM yield degradation, model circuit aging, and diagnose parametric faults \cite{makris2018special, amrouch2022machine}. Typical workflows train neural networks, decision trees, or support vector machines on features such as write delay, bitline discharge rate, and supply currents. 

However, several existing studies evaluate classifiers primarily on samples partitioned randomly from an identical nominal environment ($27^\circ\text{C}, 1.0\,\text{V}$) \cite{mukhopadhyay2007memories}. Under real-world deployment, operational variations in ambient temperature ($-40^\circ\text{C}$ to $125^\circ\text{C}$) and supply droop ($0.9\,\text{V}$ to $1.1\,\text{V}$) induce substantial physical parameter drift. When ML decision boundaries are trained on raw absolute feature magnitudes, this environmental drift mimics defect signatures, triggering severe cross-corner diagnostic degradation. While circuit designers have long utilized on-die replica tracking structures for adaptive timing and margin tracking, this work bridges that physical concept into the machine learning domain through ratiometric invariant feature normalization, demonstrating robust domain generalization across the evaluated 45-corner grid without per-corner model retraining.

\section{6T SRAM Circuit Architecture \& Physical Operations}
\label{sec:circuit}

\subsection{Transistor Sizing and Symmetrical Topology}
The circuit under investigation is a symmetrical 6T SRAM cell implemented using a representative 180\,nm CMOS SPICE benchmark rather than a proprietary foundry-qualified PDK, designed to investigate circuit-level parametric fault signatures and reference normalization under controlled PVT variations. Transistors are modeled using the foundational SPICE \texttt{LEVEL=1} (Shichman-Hodges) drift-diffusion formulation. Nominal device model parameters at $T_{\text{nom}} = 27^\circ\text{C}$ are: for NMOS devices, $V_{\text{TO},n} = 0.450\,\text{V}$, transconductance $KP_n = 250\,\mu\text{A}/\text{V}^2$, body-effect coefficient $\gamma_n = 0.4\,\text{V}^{0.5}$, channel-length modulation $\lambda_n = 0.08\,\text{V}^{-1}$, surface potential $\phi_n = 0.7\,\text{V}$, and gate oxide thickness $t_{\text{ox}} = 4.0\,\text{nm}$; for PMOS devices, $V_{\text{TO},p} = -0.450\,\text{V}$, $KP_p = 100\,\mu\text{A}/\text{V}^2$, $\gamma_p = 0.4\,\text{V}^{0.5}$, $\lambda_p = 0.10\,\text{V}^{-1}$, $\phi_p = 0.7\,\text{V}$, and $t_{\text{ox}} = 4.0\,\text{nm}$. Thermal effects across the five operating temperatures ($-40^\circ\text{C}$ to $125^\circ\text{C}$) are governed via the SPICE \texttt{.TEMP} directive, which models temperature-dependent carrier mobility degradation ($\mu(T) = \mu(T_{\text{nom}})(T/T_{\text{nom}})^{-1.5}$), intrinsic carrier density $n_i(T)$, Fermi potential $\phi_F(T)$, and junction/subthreshold leakage currents. Process corners (FF and SS) are generated by systematically modifying the netlist model cards, scaling nominal threshold voltages by $\mp 10\%$ ($V_{\text{TO}} = \pm 0.405\,\text{V}$ for FF, $\pm 0.495\,\text{V}$ for SS) and transconductance coefficients by $\pm 10\%$ ($KP \times 1.10$ for FF, $KP \times 0.90$ for SS). While native 180\,nm technologies typically operate at $1.8\,\text{V}$, the cell operates at a nominal supply voltage of $1.0\,\text{V}$ ($V_{DD} \in [0.9, 1.1]\,\text{V}$), yielding a supply-to-nominal-threshold-voltage ratio of approximately $2.22$ ($V_{DD,\text{nom}}/|V_{\text{TO}}| = 1.0/0.45$). For an NMOS transistor driven with $V_{GS} = V_{DD}$, the corresponding first-order gate overdrive is approximately $0.55\,\text{V}$, before accounting for bias-dependent threshold-voltage effects. This reduced-voltage configuration is used to study the sensitivity of SRAM operation to parametric defects and environmental variations, while leveraging the drift-diffusion formulation to isolate and evaluate first-order common-mode suppression. The cell schematic is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:cell_arch}, comprising two cross-coupled CMOS inverters ($M_1/M_3$ and $M_2/M_4$) and two NMOS access pass-gates ($M_5, M_6$) gated by the Wordline ($WL$). Symmetrical orientation is enforced across internal storage nodes $Q$ and $Q_b$ and bitlines $BL$ and $\overline{BL}$.

Device channel dimensions are sized against established memory stability criteria:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pull-down Driver NMOS ($M_3, M_4$):} $W_D = 720\,\text{nm}, L_D = 180\,\text{nm} \implies (W/L)_D = 4.0$.
    \item \textbf{Access Pass-gate NMOS ($M_5, M_6$):} $W_{AX} = 540\,\text{nm}, L_{AX} = 180\,\text{nm} \implies (W/L)_{AX} = 3.0$.
    \item \textbf{Pull-up PMOS ($M_1, M_2$):} $W_{\text{PU}} = 360\,\text{nm}, L_{\text{PU}} = 180\,\text{nm} \implies (W/L)_{\text{PU}} = 2.0$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Stability Design Criteria:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cell Ratio (CR):} The cell ratio is defined as the ratio of pull-down driver NMOS strength to access NMOS strength. For devices with identical channel lengths ($L = 180\,\text{nm}$) and identical nominal transistor parameters, the geometrical cell ratio is:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{CR} = \frac{(W/L)_{\text{driver}}}{(W/L)_{\text{access}}} = \frac{720\,\text{nm}}{540\,\text{nm}} = 1.333
    \end{equation}
    While values above $1.2$ are commonly targeted in 6T SRAM design heuristics to favor driver conduction over access conduction during read access, geometrical sizing alone does not independently guarantee read stability. Actual read stability depends on terminal biases, dynamic bitline precharge, supply voltage, and bias-dependent threshold voltages (including body effect on access pass-gates during bitline discharge), and is rigorously quantified in this work through direct Static Noise Margin (SNM) butterfly extraction (Section~\ref{sec:snm_extract}) and dynamic bump-voltage characterization ($V_{\text{bump}} = 127.9\,\text{mV} \ll V_{DD}/2$).
    \item \textbf{Pull-up Ratio (PR):} To support writeability, the pull-up ratio satisfies the geometric sizing condition:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{PR} = \frac{(W/L)_{\text{pullup}}}{(W/L)_{\text{access}}} = \frac{360\,\text{nm}}{540\,\text{nm}} = 0.667 < 1.0
    \end{equation}
    Accounting for the nominal transconductance-parameter ratio $KP_p/KP_n = 100/250 = 0.40$, the pull-up-to-access strength-parameter ratio is approximately $0.267$. This sizing favors access-device conduction relative to the pull-up transistor during a write operation (with geometric Writability Ratio $\text{WR} = 1/\text{PR} = 1.50 > 1.0$). Actual writeability is verified through the simulated transient write-flip response.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Baselines at Nominal Corner ($27^\circ\text{C}, 1.0\,\text{V}$)}
Three distinct dynamic operations and two static DC characterizations are performed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hold Mode ($WL=0\,\text{V}, BL=\overline{BL}=V_{DD}$):} Latched state retains internal nodes: $V(Q) = 1.000\,\text{V}$, $V(Q_b) = 0.000\,\text{V}$. Standby quiescent leakage current is verified at $I_{\text{DDQ}} = 4.2\,\text{nA}$ ($0.0042\,\mu\text{A}$, dominated by reverse junction leakage and off-state channel conductance in this LEVEL-1 deck).
    \item \textbf{Read 1 / Read 0 Mode:} Bitlines are precharged to $V_{DD}$ ($C_{\text{BL}} = 20\,\text{fF}$). $WL$ is stepped to $V_{DD}$. During Read 1, $Q=1$ holds $M_3$ off, while $Q_b=0$ causes driver $M_4$ and access pass-gate $M_6$ to sink bitline current, developing a differential discharge $\Delta V_{\text{BL}} = 235.4\,\text{mV}$ at strobe timing $t_{\text{sense}} = 64.5\,\text{ps}$ (where $t_{\text{sense}}$ represents the sensing strobe window, while bitline discharge dynamics are tracked via $\Delta V_{\text{BL}}$). Read disturbance bump voltage on the storage node storing zero is contained at $V_{\text{bump}} = 127.9\,\text{mV} \ll V_{DD}/2$.
    \item \textbf{Write 1 / Write 0 Mode:} Driven with differential bitlines ($BL=V_{DD}, \overline{BL}=0\,\text{V}$). Wordline assertion activates access pass-gates $M_5$ and $M_6$, allowing $M_6$ to pull $Q_b$ down against pull-up PMOS $M_2$ through $0.5 V_{DD}$, initiating regenerative latch flipping. Write flip delay $t_{\text{write}}$ (averaged across Write 1 and Write 0 operations) is measured as $t_{\text{write}} = 62.2\,\text{ps}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Numerical Inscribed-Square SNM Butterfly Extraction}
\label{sec:snm_extract}
To measure Static Noise Margin, dedicated DC testbenches are executed where the feedback loop is opened in SPICE by duplicating the cell core. A DC voltage sweep is applied from $0\,\text{V}$ to $V_{DD}$ in $1.0\,\text{mV}$ increments to trace the forward and reverse Voltage Transfer Characteristics: $V(Q_b) = f(V_Q)$ and $V(Q) = g(V_{Q_b})$.

Our extraction engine evaluates the piecewise characteristics directly via numerical interpolation. For the upper-left lobe (Lobe 1), the maximum inscribed square side length $s_1$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
s_1 = \max_{t \in [0, V_{DD}], \, s \ge 0} s \quad \text{s.t.} \quad f(g(t) + s) \ge t + s
\label{eq:snm_lobe1}
\end{equation}
and dually for the lower-right lobe (Lobe 2):
\begin{equation}
s_2 = \max_{t \in [0, V_{DD}], \, s \ge 0} s \quad \text{s.t.} \quad g(f(t) + s) \ge t + s
\label{eq:snm_lobe2}
\end{equation}
yielding $\text{SNM} = \min(s_1, s_2)$. If severe bridging defects collapse the regenerative loop such that no positive square can be inscribed, the solver assigns $0.0\,\text{V}$, naturally capturing catastrophic bistability failure.

As reported in Table~\ref{tab:snm_val}, the automated extraction script matches golden manual tangent-line measurements ($470.00\,\text{mV}$ in Hold, $263.70\,\text{mV}$ in Read) to within $0.06\,\text{mV}$ via piecewise-linear interpolation. On asymmetric faulty cells (e.g., single-sided pass-gate open defects where Read lobe asymmetry reaches $1.7\,\text{mV}$), evaluating $f$ and $g$ independently preserves lobe separability without diagonal symmetry assumptions.

\section{Parametric Fault Taxonomy \& Multi-Corner Simulation Matrix}
\label{sec:faults}

\subsection{Parametric Defect Taxonomy and Severity Tiers}
We implement a five-class defect taxonomy directly mapped to physical failure mechanisms in scaled CMOS memories via automated SPICE netlist mutation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Class 0 (Healthy Baseline):} The unperturbed golden nominal cell deck, serving as the benchmark against which invariant feature ratios equal unity by construction.
    \item \textbf{Class 1 (Resistive Open Defect):} Models partial via/contact voids or metal electromigration in the access path. Injected as a series resistance $R_{\text{open}}$ on the bitline side of access pass-gate $M_5$ or $M_6$ across six severity tiers ($S_1$ to $S_6$):
    \begin{equation}
    R_{\text{open}} \in \{0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10\}\,\text{k}\Omega
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Class 2 (Bridging Defect):} Models inter-layer dielectric breakdown or metallic sliver shorts bridging the two internal complementary storage nodes. Injected as a parallel shunt resistor $R_{\text{bridge}}$ between nodes $Q$ and $Q_b$ across five severity tiers ($S_1$ to $S_5$):
    \begin{equation}
    R_{\text{bridge}} \in \{0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10\}\,\text{k}\Omega
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Class 3 ($V_{\text{th}}$ Storage Pair Drift):} Models NBTI/PBTI interface-state trapping and channel hot-carrier degradation in the cross-coupled inverters. Injected by shifting $|V_{\text{TO}}|$ symmetrically across inverter transistors $M_1$–$M_4$ across six tiers ($S_1$ to $S_6$, $+5\%$ to $+30\%$, representing $22.5\text{--}135\,\text{mV}$ parametric shifts spanning operational wear to accelerated burn-in stress):
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta V_{\text{th,store}} \in \{+5\%, +10\%, +15\%, +20\%, +25\%, +30\%\}
    \end{equation}
    For PMOS pull-up devices ($M_1, M_2$), a $+x\%$ shift denotes an increase in magnitude $|V_{\text{th}}|$, scaling $V_{\text{TO},p}$ more negative (from $-0.450\,\text{V}$ to $-0.585\,\text{V}$), thereby compressing drive current.
    \item \textbf{Class 4 ($V_{\text{th}}$ Access Pair Drift):} Models pass-gate dielectric charge trapping on access devices $M_5$ and $M_6$. Injected by shifting $V_{\text{TO},n}$ symmetrically on $M_5/M_6$ across six severity tiers ($S_1$ to $S_6$):
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta V_{\text{th,access}} \in \{+5\%, +10\%, +15\%, +20\%, +25\%, +30\%\}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The severity tiers $S_1$ through $S_6$ map directly to discrete physical defect parameters: for Class 1, $S_1=0.2\,\text{k}\Omega$ to $S_6=10\,\text{k}\Omega$; for Class 2, $S_1=500\,\Omega$ to $S_5=10\,\text{k}\Omega$ (bridging terminates at $S_5$, so $S_6$ contains no bridging instances); and for Classes 3 and 4, $S_1=+5\%$ to $S_6=+30\%$.

\subsection{45-Corner Process-Voltage-Temperature (PVT) Operating Envelope \& Simulation Matrix}
To evaluate operational variations across the operating envelope, a 45-corner Process-Voltage-Temperature (PVT) matrix is constructed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Process Corners (3):} Typical-Typical (TT: nominal $V_{\text{TO},n} = 0.450\,\text{V}$, $KP_n = 250\,\mu\text{A}/\text{V}^2$, $V_{\text{TO},p} = -0.450\,\text{V}$, $KP_p = 100\,\mu\text{A}/\text{V}^2$), Fast-Fast (FF: $V_{\text{TO},n} = 0.405\,\text{V}$, $V_{\text{TO},p} = -0.405\,\text{V}$, $KP \times 1.10$), and Slow-Slow (SS: $V_{\text{TO},n} = 0.495\,\text{V}$, $V_{\text{TO},p} = -0.495\,\text{V}$, $KP \times 0.90$).
    \item \textbf{Supply Voltages ($V_{DD}$, 3):} $0.9\,\text{V}$ ($-10\%$), $1.0\,\text{V}$ (nominal), $1.1\,\text{V}$ ($+10\%$).
    \item \textbf{Temperatures ($T$, 5):} $-40^\circ\text{C}$ (industrial cold), $0^\circ\text{C}$, $27^\circ\text{C}$ (room nominal), $75^\circ\text{C}$, $125^\circ\text{C}$ (worst-case automotive thermal).
\end{itemize}
Combining 3 process corners $\times$ 15 Voltage-Temperature corners yields 45 distinct PVT corners without inter-device skew (FS/SF). Every corner contains 30 distinct physical sample configurations (1 Healthy, 12 Resistive Open sweeps [6 on $M_5$, 6 on $M_6$], 5 Bridging sweeps, 6 Storage $V_{\text{th}}$ sweeps, and 6 Access $V_{\text{th}}$ sweeps), yielding:
\begin{equation}
N_{\text{total}} = 30 \times 45 = 1{,}350 \text{ authentic dataset rows}
\end{equation}
For each of the 1,350 instances, our automated multithreaded simulation runner executes 7 independent SPICE operational decks (Hold, Write 1, Write 0, Read 1, Read 0, SNM Hold DC, and SNM Read DC), totaling \textbf{9,450 dynamic SPICE simulation runs}. In addition, for each of the 45 corners, a dedicated healthy reference simulation is executed under identical environmental and process parameters (315 additional runs, bringing total SPICE simulations to 9,765) to provide the per-corner normalization baseline. Raw waveform outputs are parsed on the fly and pruned, maintaining repository footprint below 50\,MB.

\subsection{Data Handling, Numerical Imputation, and Sensitivity to Solver Timeouts}
\label{sec:timeouts}
A rigorous automated simulation audit tracked solver stability across all 45 corners: 1,327 of 1,350 samples converged cleanly (98.3\% overall convergence rate: 450/450 in FF [100\%], 449/450 in SS [99.8\%], and 428/450 in TT [95.1\%]). Crucially, of the simulation warnings observed during DC butterfly extraction, the vast majority ($>92\%$) correspond to non-monotonic warnings arising exclusively in severe Class 2 internal bridging defects ($R_{\text{bridge}} \le 1\,\text{k}\Omega$) and extreme $V_{\text{th}}$ shifts. In these severe fault states, the low-impedance shunt between nodes $Q$ and $Q_b$ collapses regenerative latch feedback, physically eliminating the bistable butterfly lobes. This represents an authentic semiconductor physical failure signature of latch destruction rather than numerical non-convergence of the SPICE engine.

True transient integration time-step timeouts occurred in only 23 runs across the entire 1,350 dataset (1.70\%: 22 in TT, 1 in SS, 0 in FF). To maintain a complete, reproducible matrix without arbitrary sample discarding, we implemented conservative physical feature imputation for these 23 instances:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dynamic timing metrics ($t_{\text{write}}$, $t_{\text{sense}}$) were clamped to the upper physical integration ceiling $\tau_{\max} = 10{,}000\,\text{ps}$ ($10\,\text{ns}$).
    \item Binary functional stability pass flags were set to 0, indicating failure to complete switching or margin evaluation within the transient integration window.
    \item Standby leakage in 2 Hold-mode timeout instances was clamped to $10 \times I_{\text{DDQ,ref}}$, reflecting degraded high-leakage fault states.
\end{itemize}
The resulting master dataset contains exactly 1,350 rows $\times$ 34 columns with \textbf{zero null, missing, or NaN values}.

To verify that the high diagnostic accuracy is not an artifact of numerical imputation, we conducted a rigorous sensitivity analysis by evaluating the Random Forest classifier under 45-corner LOCO on the strictly converged subset ($N = 1{,}327$, completely omitting all 23 timeout instances). Under converged-only evaluation, LOCO classification accuracy increases from $99.33\%$ (1,341/1,350) to \textbf{99.70\%} (1,323/1,327), with total misclassifications across all 45 corners dropping from 9 down to only 4. Balanced accuracy and Macro-F1 both increase to \textbf{99.70\%} and \textbf{99.76\%}, respectively. The number of perfectly diagnosed corners (100.0\% accuracy on unseen withheld corners) rises from 39/45 to \textbf{43 of 45 corners}, while the worst-case corner accuracy floor remains at \textbf{90.00\%}. This confirms that our imputation strategy is conservative and slightly penalizes the model, and that high cross-PVT diagnostic performance is driven by genuine physical feature separability.

\section{Physics-Normalized Feature Engineering}
\label{sec:features}

\subsection{Extracted Dynamic and Static Features}
For every cell instance $i$ simulated at corner $c = (\text{Process}, V_{DD}, T)$, we extract dynamic transient metrics and static margin metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Timing Delays:} $t_{\text{write}}$ (time from $50\%$ $WL$ rise until internal node reaches $0.5 V_{DD}$) and $t_{\text{sense}}$ (time to develop $\Delta V_{\text{BL}}$).
    \item \textbf{Currents \& Voltages:} $I_{\text{DDQ}}$ (quiescent standby current), $I_{\text{write,peak}}$ (peak switching current), $\Delta V_{\text{BL}}$ (bitline strobe differential), and $V_{\text{bump}}$ (internal read disturbance).
    \item \textbf{Stability Margins:} $\text{SNM}_{\text{hold}}$, $\text{SNM}_{\text{read}}$, asymmetry $\Delta \text{SNM}$, and binary stability pass flags.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Formulation of Invariant Ratios}
The core innovation of our methodology is the cancellation of environmental shifts through \textit{concurrent reference-cell normalization}. An on-die golden cell located adjacent to the array experiences the identical instantaneous process parameters, supply voltage $V_{DD}$, and ambient temperature $T$. Importantly, this architecture enables \textit{zero-recalibration cross-PVT transfer}: the trained machine learning classifier requires zero post-deployment calibration, corner-specific fine-tuning, or on-chip environmental sensor telemetry when operating across unseen corners, because common-mode environmental tracking is performed dynamically in the analog feature domain by referencing against the local tracking cell. We define the invariant feature vector $\mathbf{X}_{\text{inv}}$ as:
\begin{align}
\text{iddq\_norm} &= \frac{I_{\text{DDQ,meas}}(c)}{I_{\text{DDQ,ref}}(c)} \\
\text{t\_write\_norm} &= \frac{t_{\text{write,meas}}(c)}{t_{\text{write,ref}}(c)} \\
\text{dv\_bl\_norm} &= \frac{\Delta V_{\text{BL,meas}}(c)}{\Delta V_{\text{BL,ref}}(c)} \\
\text{snm\_hold\_norm} &= \frac{\text{SNM}_{\text{hold,meas}}(c)}{\text{SNM}_{\text{hold,ref}}(c)} \\
\text{snm\_read\_norm} &= \frac{\text{SNM}_{\text{read,meas}}(c)}{\text{SNM}_{\text{read,ref}}(c)}
\end{align}
Additionally, we define dimensionless internal electrical invariants:
\begin{align}
\text{delay\_ratio} &= \frac{t_{\text{sense}}}{t_{\text{write}}}, \quad
\text{snm\_ratio} = \frac{\text{SNM}_{\text{read}}}{\text{SNM}_{\text{hold}}} \\
\text{dv\_vdd\_ratio} &= \frac{\Delta V_{\text{BL}}}{V_{DD}}
\end{align}

\subsection{Interpretation of Reference Normalization and Dispersion Suppression}
In deterministic SPICE simulations with identical transistor model cards, the normalized feature of an unperturbed healthy cell equals unity ($X_{\text{norm}} \equiv 1.0$) when its electrical response is referenced against an identical healthy cell simulated at the same operating condition. This equality is a direct algebraic consequence of quotient normalization ($X_{\text{meas}}/X_{\text{ref}}$) and confirms common-mode environmental cancellation.

As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:spread_comp} and Fig.~\ref{fig:dispersion}, quotient division algebraically cancels common-mode multiplicative voltage droop ($V_{DD}$), process drive shifts, and exponential thermal scaling factors across the entire 45-corner envelope:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Standby Current ($I_{\text{DDQ}}$):} Driven by exponential thermal scaling ($119.5\times$ spread from $27^\circ\text{C}$ to $125^\circ\text{C}$ in TT 1.0\,V, spanning $0.0007\,\mu\text{A}$ to $3.902\,\mu\text{A}$ across the 45-corner grid), raw $I_{\text{DDQ}}$ exhibits an extreme $\text{CV} = 197.59\%$ ($0.327 \pm 0.645\,\mu\text{A}$). On clean physical extractions across 43 corners, normalized $I_{\text{DDQ}}$ achieves $\mu \pm \sigma = 1.000 \pm 0.000$ ($\text{CV} = \mathbf{0.00\%}$), eliminating common-mode environmental dispersion by \textbf{100.0\%} (and 43.42\% on the full 45-corner dataset including the two high-temperature transient integration timeouts clamped to 10.0).
    \item \textbf{Hold SNM:} Raw CV is $12.40\%$ ($0.437 \pm 0.054\,\text{V}$); normalized Hold SNM achieves $\text{CV} = \mathbf{0.00\%}$, eliminating environmental dispersion by \textbf{100.0\%}.
    \item \textbf{Bitline Discharge ($\Delta V_{\text{BL}}$):} Raw CV is $38.50\%$ ($239.59 \pm 92.25\,\text{mV}$); normalized $\Delta V_{\text{BL}}$ achieves $\text{CV} = \mathbf{0.00\%}$ (\textbf{100.0\% reduction}).
    \item \textbf{Write Delay ($t_{\text{write}}$):} Raw CV is $18.30\%$ ($61.74 \pm 11.30\,\text{ps}$); normalized write delay drops to $\text{CV} = \mathbf{3.49\%}$ ($1.005 \pm 0.035$), achieving an \textbf{80.91\% dispersion reduction}.
    \item \textbf{Read SNM:} Raw CV is $22.10\%$ ($0.233 \pm 0.051\,\text{V}$); normalized Read SNM drops to $\text{CV} = \mathbf{15.25\%}$ (\textbf{31.02\% reduction}).
\end{itemize}
The true diagnostic value of the proposed methodology is therefore evaluated through: (1) multi-class classification of \textit{faulty} cells across held-out PVT corners (Section~\ref{sec:results}), and (2) sensitivity to local reference measurement perturbations and instrumentation noise (Section~\ref{sec:results}-D).

\section{Experimental Results \& Cross-PVT Evaluation}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocols}
We establish two distinct evaluation protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Protocol 1 (Standard Nominal Split):} In-distribution nominal data only (TT nominal corner $27^\circ\text{C}, 1.0\,\text{V}$, 30 samples). Standard 80/20 train/test split (24 training samples, 6 testing samples, fixed random state 42). Because Class 0 (Healthy baseline) comprises exactly one reference sample at the nominal corner, standard stratification across all 5 classes is mathematically constrained; the single healthy sample is allocated to the training split, and the test split evaluates the remaining 6 held-out defect instances across Classes 1--4. Thus, Protocol 1 assesses inter-fault classification among defect classes under nominal conditions rather than healthy-state recall, serving strictly as an in-distribution sanity check, whereas true out-of-distribution generalization and healthy recall are assessed under Protocol 2.
    \item \textbf{Protocol 2 (Leave-One-Corner-Out / LOCO):} True cross-domain evaluation. The model trains on 44 corners (1,320 samples) and is evaluated exclusively on the remaining 45th unseen corner (30 samples). This process is iterated for all 45 corners, evaluating all 1,350 distinct cell instances.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cross-PVT LOCO Diagnostic Results}
Under Protocol 1 (in-distribution nominal split), all evaluated machine learning models achieve \textbf{100.0\% accuracy} on both raw and invariant features, correctly classifying all 6 held-out nominal defect instances across Classes 1--4 and serving as an initial sanity check that defect classes are separable under static lab conditions.

However, under Protocol 2 (LOCO cross-PVT across all 45 corners), the diagnostic collapse of raw features becomes immediately apparent. As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:loco_master} and Fig.~\ref{fig:loco_bars}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic Collapse on Raw Features:} Random Forest accuracy on raw metrics degrades to $75.11\% \pm 11.23\%$, collapsing to an unacceptable \textbf{50.00\%} floor on held-out $0.9\,\text{V}, -40^\circ\text{C}$ corners (specifically SS $0.9\,\text{V}, -40^\circ\text{C}$ and TT $0.9\,\text{V}, -40^\circ\text{C}$, with HistGradientBoosting dropping to $40.00\%$ and Decision Trees dropping to $36.67\%$). Because each corner evaluates 29 faulty cells and only 1 healthy cell, the collapse is driven primarily by \textbf{inter-fault confusion}: at $0.9\,\text{V}$ under slow or cold conditions, higher process $V_{\text{th}}$, low supply headroom, and thermal built-in potential drift at $-40^\circ\text{C}$ compress gate overdrive ($V_{GS} - V_{\text{th}}$), expanding dynamic write delays to their global maximum ($87.6\,\text{ps}$) identically to series contact resistance. At this $50.00\%$ floor (15 errors out of 30 samples, compared to $53.33\text{--}86.67\%$ across other $0.9\,\text{V}$ corners), the raw classifier misclassifies 100\% of access pass-gate resistive opens (12 of 12) as access-transistor $V_{\text{th}}$ aging drift, while 2 storage $V_{\text{th}}$ drift samples and the 1 healthy evaluation cell are likewise misdiagnosed as access $V_{\text{th}}$ drift (accounting for all 15 errors). Bridging faults (which draw up to $66.94\,\mu\text{A}$ with a median of $5.49\,\mu\text{A}$, dropping to $0.059\,\mu\text{A}$ only when weak inverters in slow corners clamp internal nodes near metastability) largely exceed co-located healthy standby currents and are isolated by functional flags, but raw decision boundaries between resistive opens, access aging, and storage aging become severely confounded.
    \item \textbf{Generalization Restoration via Invariant Features:} Random Forest and HistGradientBoosting trained on invariant features achieve \textbf{99.33\%} ($1341/1350$ correct across the pooled test set) and \textbf{99.41\%} ($1342/1350$ correct) mean accuracy, delivering a \textbf{$+24.22\%$} and \textbf{$+21.85\%$} generalization gain over raw features. Most importantly, the worst-corner accuracy floor is elevated from 50.00\% to \textbf{90.00\%} (RF) and from 40.00\% to \textbf{86.67\%} (HistGB), with \textbf{39 of 45 corners achieving perfect 100.0\% accuracy} for RF and \textbf{40 of 45 corners} for HistGB.
    \item \textbf{Linear vs Non-Linear Classifiers:} Decision Tree accuracy improves by $+24.89\%$ (from $73.56\%$ to $98.44\%$). In contrast, linear Logistic Regression improves by only $+6.30\%$ ($86.89\%$). The non-linear tree ensembles substantially outperform linear models because multi-class defect boundaries involve non-linear ratio interactions (e.g., decoupling Class 3 Storage from Class 4 Access $V_{\text{th}}$ shifts).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Controlled Normalization Operator Ablation}
A fundamental question in domain-invariant testing is the mathematical choice of the normalization operator: \textit{Why is quotient normalization ($X_{\text{meas}}/X_{\text{ref}}$) superior to classical subtraction ($X_{\text{meas}} - X_{\text{ref}}$)?}

To isolate this effect rigorously, we conducted a controlled 1-to-1 ablation keeping the sample set (1,350 instances), classifier architecture (Random Forest, 100 estimators), and validation folds identical across all 45 corners on the core electrical features:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Matched Raw Features (5 Base):} Achieves $73.48\%$ accuracy, collapsing to $36.67\%$ at extreme corners.
    \item \textbf{Matched Difference ($\Delta X = X_{\text{meas}} - X_{\text{ref}}$):} Elevates accuracy to $98.52\%$, but still suffers corner degradation down to $80.00\%$.
    \item \textbf{Matched Log-Ratio ($\ln(X_{\text{meas}} / X_{\text{ref}})$):} Achieves \textbf{99.04\%} accuracy with an $83.33\%$ corner floor.
    \item \textbf{Matched Ratio ($X_{\text{meas}} / X_{\text{ref}}$):} Achieves \textbf{99.11\%} accuracy with an $83.33\%$ corner floor.
\end{enumerate}

This disparity is directly grounded in semiconductor device physics. Dynamic switching delay scales as $t_{\text{write}} \propto \frac{C_{\text{load}} V_{DD}}{K' (V_{DD} - V_{\text{th}})^\alpha}$; supply droop ($0.9\,\text{V}$) and process mobility variations act multiplicatively on delay. Quotient division cancels these common-mode multiplicative factors. In our simulated benchmark, quiescent standby current $I_{\text{DDQ}}$ acts as a composite leakage surrogate demonstrating an empirical $119.5\times$ thermal expansion. Physically, in scaled technologies where subthreshold channel conduction dominates, leakage follows the exponential relationship $I_{\text{sub}} \propto \mu(T) T^2 \exp\left(\frac{V_{GS} - V_{\text{th}}}{m k T / q}\right)$. Under such exponential scaling, a linear difference $(I_{\text{meas}} - I_{\text{ref}}) = I_{\text{ref}} \left(\exp(-\Delta V_{\text{th}} / (mkT/q)) - 1\right)$ retains an explicit multiplicative dependence on baseline leakage $I_{\text{ref}}$, which shifts across the thermal envelope by over two orders of magnitude. Both ratio and log-ratio transformations analytically isolate the parametric shift from common-mode thermal scaling, explaining why ratio and log-ratio operators dramatically outperform subtraction. Further augmenting the 5 basic ratios with internal electrical invariants ($\text{delay\_ratio}, \text{snm\_ratio}, \text{dv\_vdd\_ratio}$) lifts LOCO accuracy to \textbf{99.48\%} and elevates the worst-corner floor to \textbf{90.00\%}. In sharp contrast, Model D (Functional Flags Only) achieves only $57.70\%$ accuracy (collapsing to a $30.00\%$ floor). While digital pass/fail flags capture hard catastrophic bistability destruction in low-resistance bridging faults, they exhibit near-zero diagnostic resolution for the $77.32\%$ of parametric defect instances that pass all functional checks, proving that digital flags alone cannot diagnose latent parametric degradation.

\subsection{Parametric-Only Diagnosis Without Functional Status Flags}
A critical concern in parametric fault diagnosis is whether the classifier relies on digital functional shortcuts (e.g., detecting severe bridging faults solely because a cell fails a binary bistability or retention flag). To establish that the proposed framework diagnoses latent parametric defects that pass functional tests, we evaluated a \textbf{purely parametric model} with all four functional status indicators (\texttt{hold\_pass}, \texttt{write\_pass}, \texttt{read\_stable\_pass}, and \texttt{is\_bistable}) completely removed, retaining only the continuous analog ratios. Crucially, recall that $93.43\%$ of all resistive-open and $V_{\text{th}}$-drift defect instances pass all four functional flags; diagnosing these latent defects requires continuous analog sensitivity.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and the pooled confusion matrix in Fig.~\ref{fig:confusion_matrix}, the parametric-only model (Model B') achieves \textbf{99.48\% accuracy}, \textbf{99.15\% balanced accuracy}, and a \textbf{99.20\% Macro-F1 score} across all 1,350 authentic SPICE samples under 45-corner LOCO, outperforming even the 12-feature model ($99.33\%$). For the full 12-feature model, per-class recall across all 1,350 instances reaches:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Class 0 (Healthy Baseline):} $97.78\%$ (44 of 45 correct; exactly 1 instance misattributed as a mild open at the extreme $-40^\circ\text{C}, 0.9\,\text{V}$ corner).

\subsection{Tolerance to Reference Measurement Perturbations and Instrument Error}
\label{sec:tolerance}
In physical silicon deployment, on-chip measurement circuitry, sensing amplifiers, and local tracking structures are subject to finite instrument precision, analog sensor noise, and thermal jitter. To evaluate model resilience against real-world measurement uncertainties, we conduct a systematic sensitivity analysis by injecting additive Gaussian noise $\Delta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ into all reference measurements ($X_{\text{ref,noisy}} = X_{\text{ref}} \cdot (1 + \Delta)$), with noise standard deviation $\sigma$ swept from $0\%$ to $15\%$. We emphasize that this represents an empirical sensitivity study to scalar reference measurement error and instrumentation tolerances, distinct from transistor-level Pelgrom threshold mismatch (which is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\begin{itemize}
    \item At typical analog sensing noise levels ($\sigma = 1\%$ to $2\%$), mean LOCO accuracy remains exceptionally high at $97.85\%$ and $96.89\%$, with worst-corner floors of $86.67\%$ and $83.33\%$.
    \item Under moderate measurement perturbation ($\sigma = 5.0\%$), the model retains $94.22\%$ cross-corner accuracy (floor $80.00\%$).
    \item Under extreme $\pm 15.0\%$ reference measurement error, the model achieves $90.67\%$ accuracy with a $56.67\%$ floor.

Crucially, even under severe $\pm 15\%$ reference measurement noise, the normalized model ($90.67\%$) retains a large $+17.19\%$ diagnostic margin over raw unnormalized features ($73.48\%$). This confirms that common-mode environmental drift across operating corners (which causes $>100\times$ shifts in raw metrics) far exceeds instrumentation noise, ensuring decisive diagnostic superiority under realistic measurement tolerances.

\subsection{Joint Voltage-Temperature and Defect Severity Holdout (Experiment C)}
To impose a stringent multi-axis stress test, we evaluated a joint holdout benchmark where \textit{neither the PVT corner nor the defect severity level was represented during model training}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Across each of the 45 folds, the complete target PVT corner was withheld, and intermediate severities ($S_3$ and $S_4$) were simultaneously stripped from all training corners.
    \item The classifier was trained exclusively on the remaining 44 corners using boundary severities ($S_0, S_1, S_2, S_5, S_6$) and evaluated on the 45th unseen corner strictly on the unseen intermediate severities ($S_3, S_4$, 450 instances).
\end{itemize}

Evaluating under these stringent conditions across all 450 held-out samples confirms that the parametric model achieves \textbf{99.11\% overall accuracy} ($446/450$) and \textbf{99.03\% balanced accuracy}. These results demonstrate that continuous analog ratio normalization alone achieves robust 99.11\% joint generalization across unseen operating conditions and unseen defect severities simultaneously.

\section{Stated Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

To maintain rigorous scientific standards, we note the explicit boundaries of this investigation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operational PVT Grid vs. Microscopic Transistor Mismatch:} In this work, operational environmental and process variation is systematically modeled across 45 Process-Voltage-Temperature operating corners in a representative 180\,nm CMOS SPICE benchmark (TT, FF, SS process corners $\times$ 15 V-T conditions), complemented by empirical reference measurement tolerance studies (Section~\ref{sec:tolerance}). In physical silicon, microscopic intra-die random device mismatch (e.g., Pelgrom-scaled threshold-voltage variations $\sigma(V_{\text{th}}) = A_{V_{\text{th}}} / \sqrt{W \cdot L}$) introduces non-linear stochastic offsets between adjacent transistors; full physical layout-extracted Monte Carlo array characterization represents an important direction for silicon tape-out.
    \item \textbf{Simulation vs Physical Silicon:} All evaluations are conducted using multi-threaded SPICE netlists. While SPICE models provide golden analog precision, physical silicon tape-out introduces unmodeled parasitics, power-grid IR drops, and thermal gradients across large memory arrays.
    \item \textbf{Reference Cell Health and Area Overhead:} The proposed methodology assumes the availability of an on-die healthy reference cell. In practical deployment, reference cell health is established during initial wafer-sort test screening, or via a fault-tolerant triplet of tracking cells with median voting to guard against random local manufacturing defects. In a typical memory macro (e.g., a 1\,Mb array of $1024 \times 1024$ cells), reserving a dedicated tracking cell pair requires $2 / 1{,}048{,}576 \approx 0.00019\%$ of the cell array area. Even in a modular 64\,Kb sub-bank ($256 \times 256$), allocating one tracking cell incurs only $1 / 65{,}536 \approx 0.0015\%$ overhead, well below $0.01\%$.
    \item \textbf{Target Test Architecture:} The extraction of high-resolution analog parameters ($1\,\text{mV}$ DC butterfly sweeps and sub-100ps internal timings) is tailored to post-silicon characterization test vehicles, dedicated scribe-line test structures, and yield-learning macros equipped with analog monitor pass-gates, rather than dense high-volume production array March testing where internal node access is physically constrained.
    \item \textbf{Technology Node \& Device Model Scope:} The 180\,nm \texttt{LEVEL=1} Shichman-Hodges model cleanly isolates fundamental drift-diffusion overdrive and simulated standby quiescent current scaling ($119.5\times$ over $27^\circ\text{C}$ to $125^\circ\text{C}$, capturing composite junction, subthreshold, and numerical leakage paths rather than the ideal unconstrained diode law $T^{XTI}\exp(-E_g/kT) \sim 10^5$), enabling rigorous verification of first-order common-mode suppression without confounding empirical tuning parameters. In nanoscale CMOS nodes, standby currents are additionally dominated by subthreshold channel conduction ($I_{\text{sub}} \propto \mu(T)T^2\exp(-qV_{\text{th}}/(mkT))$); because both mechanisms scale exponentially with temperature, ratiometric normalization ($I_{\text{DDQ,meas}}/I_{\text{DDQ,ref}}$) cancels common-mode thermal dispersion effectively across both domains. In sub-20\,nm FinFET or Gate-All-Around (GAA) architectures, total quiescent current ($I_{\text{DDQ}}$) additionally encompasses gate-oxide direct tunneling ($I_{\text{gate}}$) and Gate-Induced Drain Leakage (GIDL), which exhibit much weaker temperature dependence than subthreshold conduction. While dynamic switching and bitline discharge invariants ($t_{\text{write,norm}}$, $\Delta V_{\text{BL,norm}}$) remain robust under scaled overdrive physics, extending current-ratiometric validation to advanced BSIM-CMG models with multi-component leakage and microscopic atomistic variations (Random Dopant Fluctuation, Line-Edge Roughness) constitutes an essential direction for future work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we addressed the critical challenge of cross-environmental and process diagnostic collapse in machine-learning-based SRAM parametric fault diagnosis. We showed that classical classifiers trained on raw parametric metrics suffer severe out-of-distribution failure when operating across realistic process, voltage, and temperature envelopes, dropping to 50.00\% accuracy (and 40.00\% for gradient boosted trees). To solve this, we proposed a physics-normalized invariant feature methodology that cancels common-mode environmental shifts using on-die concurrent reference normalization. 

Through an automated SPICE harness executing 9,450 dynamic simulation runs plus 315 reference runs across a 45-corner Process-Voltage-Temperature grid (3 process corners $\times$ 3 supply voltages $\times$ 5 temperatures) on a low-voltage 180\,nm CMOS benchmark, we evaluated invariant feature normalization, showing that it eliminates environmental dispersion by up to 100.0\% on healthy cells. Under rigorous Leave-One-Corner-Out (LOCO) benchmarking across all 45 corners (1,350 authentic SPICE samples), the proposed invariant formulation elevated Random Forest and HistGradientBoosting classification accuracy from 75.11\% and 77.56\% to 99.33\% and 99.41\% ($+24.22\%$ and $+21.85\%$ gain), lifting the worst-corner diagnostic floor from 50.00\% and 40.00\% to 90.00\% and 86.67\%. Crucially, Leave-One-Severity-Out (LOSO) interpolation across intermediate severities ($S_2$–$S_5$, $99.54\%$, with $90.74\%$ on incipient $S_1$ defects) and joint holdout simultaneously withholding corners and intermediate severities demonstrated $99.11\%$ accuracy, demonstrating robust multi-axis generalization without post-deployment recalibration. Tree SHAP attributions corroborated that decision boundaries align with underlying device physics. This framework establishes the foundational feasibility of ratiometric invariant feature normalization for embedded memory diagnostic workflows in scaled, low-power CMOS SoCs.
