\begin{abstract}
Process, voltage, and temperature (PVT) variation can obscure electrical signatures of parametric SRAM faults. We evaluate five-class diagnosis in a 6T SRAM SPICE benchmark using reference-normalized electrical measurements and internal feature ratios. The benchmark comprises 1,350 observations over 45 combinations of process setting, supply voltage, and temperature. In leave-one-corner-out evaluation, the normalized feature set improves Random Forest accuracy from 75.11\% to 99.33\% and raises the worst-corner accuracy from 50.00\% to 90.00\%. Excluding 23 rows marked nonconverged before splitting gives 75.28\% accuracy with raw features and 99.70\% with the normalized set. A joint holdout of operating corner and interior fault-parameter levels yields 99.11\% accuracy on 450 faulty observations. At parameter boundaries, the $S_1$ holdout yields 88.89\% overall across the tested faults, including 76.67\% on the weakest tested resistive opens. This tier includes the strongest tested bridges. At a fixed 5\,k$\Omega$ open on $M_5$, normalization reduces cross-corner write-delay CV from 15.05\% to 3.66\% and bitline-swing CV from 36.38\% to 2.28\% over 43 corners with converged observations. The results quantify the benefit of a matched healthy reference across the simulated operating grid.

\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
6T SRAM, parametric fault diagnosis, process-voltage-temperature variation, reference normalization, static noise margin, machine learning.

\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Embedded SRAM is an important contributor to the area, yield, and reliability of memory-intensive integrated circuits. Statistical SRAM design and failure analysis must account for device and operating-condition variation \cite{mukhopadhyay2005modeling}. We evaluate how global process parameters, supply voltage, and temperature affect the classification of electrical defect signatures.

Parametric variation affects SRAM timing and stability \cite{mukhopadhyay2007memories}. This study models resistive opens, internal node bridges, and threshold-voltage shifts as distinct fault classes. Their electrical signatures depend on the affected device and operating condition. For example, reduced access drive can slow bitline discharge while reducing the read disturbance on the storage node. Of the 1,080 open and threshold-shift observations, 1,009 pass the four screening checks used here (hold, write, read stability, and bistability). Electrical measurements can help distinguish defects that produce the same pass/fail outcome under these checks.

We investigate whether a matched healthy reference helps a classifier distinguish defect signatures from shifts in operating conditions. The feature set includes write delay, bitline swing, static noise margin (SNM), and sampled standby supply current.

Operating conditions affect several diagnostic measurements at once. In the leave-one-corner-out (LOCO) evaluation, the raw-feature Random Forest reaches its 50.00\% accuracy floor at both TT and SS, 0.9\,V, $-40^\circ$C. Each corner has 29 faulty configurations and one healthy configuration, so confusion among fault classes accounts for most errors at these corners.

We normalize target measurements against a healthy reference simulated under the same process setting, supply voltage, and temperature. This gives the classifier a common basis for comparing electrical signatures across corners. The contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Automated Multi-Operation SPICE Simulation Harness:} A Python framework that injects four classes of parametric defects at discrete parameter levels and runs hold, write, read, and DC characterizations of a 6T SRAM cell across 45 PVT corners.
    \item \textbf{Numerical SNM Butterfly Extraction:} A direct numerical inscribed-square search performed on unrotated voltage transfer characteristics to estimate Hold and Read Static Noise Margins. The method avoids reliance on simplified peak-separation approximations and permits explicit evaluation of asymmetric or distorted butterfly lobes.
    \item \textbf{Reference-Normalized Features.} We combine five ratios to a matched healthy reference with three internal ratios. A fixed-resistance open comparison quantifies how much dispersion remains across corners.
    \item \textbf{Cross-PVT and Parameter Holdouts.} We evaluate the 45 operating corners and test how classification changes when defect-parameter values are also held out.
    \item \textbf{Model Interpretation via Tree SHAP:} Global and class-specific attributions describe feature contributions to the fitted Random Forest's predictions. We interpret the patterns in the context of access-path and latch behavior.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work \& Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Traditional SRAM Fault Testing}
Conventional SRAM testing commonly employs March algorithms, including March C- and March SS, executed via digital Built-In Self-Test (BIST) engines to detect functional faults such as stuck-at, transition, coupling, and dynamic faults \cite{vandegoor1993march, vandegoor2000functional, hamdioui2003dynamic}. Although these algorithms provide effective coverage for their targeted functional fault models, certain parametric defects may remain undetected when the resulting degradation does not violate the logic or timing requirements of the applied test sequence. For example, moderate resistive opens or transistor threshold-voltage shifts may reduce writeability, read stability, or timing margins while preserving successful digital read and write operations under nominal conditions. Specialized Design-for-Testability (DFT) schemes, such as the programmable weak-cell detection threshold technique developed by Pavlov, Sachdev, and Pineda de Gyvez \cite{pavlov2004sram}, have been proposed to detect margin-compromised cells. Nonetheless, comprehensive multi-class physical diagnosis and process yield learning benefit from high-resolution analog characterization to complement conventional March testing by revealing degradation not observable through the selected functional test conditions.

Sabade and Walker compared $\Delta I_{\mathrm{DDQ}}$ and current-ratio screening on industrial test data \cite{sabade2004comparison}. The delta test uses differences between successive current readings on a die. The ratio test relates its maximum and minimum currents across test vectors. We use a healthy cell at the same PVT corner as the reference for sampled standby current, write delay, bitline swing, and noise margins. A bridge between complementary storage nodes provides a current path whose contribution depends on the resistance and the resulting node voltages.

\subsection{Static Noise Margin Extraction Methodologies}
The foundational standard for SRAM stability analysis was formulated by Seevinck, List, and Lohstroh \cite{seevinck1987static}. Static Noise Margin (SNM) quantifies the maximum tolerable DC noise disturbance before a bistable SRAM cell loses its stored state. In the butterfly-curve method, the Voltage Transfer Characteristics (VTCs) of the two cross-coupled inverters are superimposed to form two closed lobes. The SNM is obtained from the smaller of the maximum square side lengths that can be inscribed within these lobes, with square edges oriented parallel to the original voltage coordinate axes:
\begin{equation}
\text{SNM} = \min(s_1, s_2)
\label{eq:snm_def}
\end{equation}
where $s_1$ and $s_2$ denote the maximum inscribed-square side lengths for the upper-left and lower-right butterfly lobes, respectively.

Crucially, cell stability must be distinguished across operational regimes. In \textbf{Hold mode} ($WL=0\,\text{V}$), access pass-gates are cut off, and Hold SNM (HSNM) characterizes the intrinsic retention stability of the isolated cross-coupled inverters. In \textbf{Read mode} ($WL=V_{DD}, BL=\overline{BL}=V_{DD}$), conducting access transistors couple the precharged bitlines to the internal storage nodes, pulling up the node storing logic ``0'' by a disturb bump voltage ($V_{\text{bump}}$) and shifting the inverter transfer characteristics inward. This compresses the enclosed butterfly lobes, which typically reduces the read margin of a healthy cell. Read SNM describes its stability under the applied read bias.

Although the $45^\circ$ coordinate transformation is mathematically exact ($u = (V_Q - V_{Qb})/\sqrt{2}$, $v = (V_Q + V_{Qb})/\sqrt{2}$), simplified extraction methods that estimate SNM from selected curve separations may introduce geometric approximations. Such approximations require care when the butterfly lobes are strongly distorted or asymmetric. The proposed method instead searches directly for the largest admissible axis-aligned square in each lobe of the original voltage-transfer characteristics.

\subsection{Machine Learning in VLSI Diagnosis and Domain Shift}
Machine learning has been studied for semiconductor test, fault diagnosis, post-silicon validation, and yield optimization \cite{makris2018special, amrouch2022machine}. Venkatesan \emph{et al.} investigated deep neural networks for detecting and classifying faults from simulated SRAM circuit responses \cite{venkatesan2024sram}.

This study tests matched-corner reference normalization for conventional classifiers of simulated SRAM defects. The feature comparison evaluates ratios and unnormalized inputs with the same reference values. The joint holdout withholds an operating corner and selected defect-parameter levels together.

\section{6T SRAM Circuit Architecture \& Physical Operations}
\label{sec:circuit}

\subsection{Transistor Sizing and Symmetrical Topology}
The symmetric 6T SRAM benchmark uses generic \texttt{LEVEL=1} Shichman--Hodges MOSFET models with 180\,nm channel lengths. Nominal parameters are $V_{\mathrm{TO},n}=0.450$\,V, $KP_n=250\,\mu$A/V$^2$, and $\lambda_n=0.08$\,V$^{-1}$ for NMOS, and $V_{\mathrm{TO},p}=-0.450$\,V, $KP_p=100\,\mu$A/V$^2$, and $\lambda_p=0.10$\,V$^{-1}$ for PMOS. Both models use $\gamma=0.4$\,V$^{0.5}$, $\phi=0.7$\,V, and $t_{\mathrm{ox}}=4$\,nm. The \texttt{.TEMP} directive invokes the simulator's temperature-dependent device equations. FF scales $|V_{\mathrm{TO}}|$ by 0.90 and $KP$ by 1.10, while SS uses 1.10 and 0.90. These are heuristic global process settings. The supply is 1.0\,V nominal, with a 0.9--1.1\,V sweep. In Fig.~\ref{fig:cell_arch}, the inverter driven by $Q$ comprises $M_1$ and $M_4$ and drives $Q_b$. The inverter driven by $Q_b$ comprises $M_2$ and $M_3$ and drives $Q$. Access transistors $M_5$ and $M_6$ connect $Q$ to $BL$ and $Q_b$ to $\overline{BL}$, respectively.

Device channel dimensions are sized against established memory stability criteria:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pull-down Driver NMOS ($M_3, M_4$):} $W_D = 720\,\text{nm}, L_D = 180\,\text{nm} \implies (W/L)_D = 4.0$.
    \item \textbf{Access Pass-gate NMOS ($M_5, M_6$):} $W_{AX} = 540\,\text{nm}, L_{AX} = 180\,\text{nm} \implies (W/L)_{AX} = 3.0$.
    \item \textbf{Pull-up PMOS ($M_1, M_2$):} $W_{\text{PU}} = 360\,\text{nm}, L_{\text{PU}} = 180\,\text{nm} \implies (W/L)_{\text{PU}} = 2.0$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Stability Design Criteria:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cell Ratio (CR):} The cell ratio is defined as the ratio of pull-down driver NMOS strength to access NMOS strength. For devices with identical channel lengths ($L = 180\,\text{nm}$) and identical nominal transistor parameters, the geometrical cell ratio is:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{CR} = \frac{(W/L)_{\text{driver}}}{(W/L)_{\text{access}}} = \frac{720\,\text{nm}}{540\,\text{nm}} = 1.333
    \end{equation}
    While values above $1.2$ are commonly targeted in 6T SRAM design heuristics to favor driver conduction over access conduction during read access, geometrical sizing alone does not independently guarantee read stability. Read stability depends on terminal biases, dynamic bitline precharge, supply voltage, and bias-dependent threshold voltages, including body effect in the access devices during bitline discharge. We characterize read stability using butterfly-curve read SNM (Section~\ref{sec:snm_extract}) and the simulated read-disturb bump ($V_{\text{bump}}=125.0\,\text{mV}$ at the nominal corner).
    \item \textbf{Pull-up Ratio (PR):} To support writeability, the pull-up ratio satisfies the geometric sizing condition:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{PR} = \frac{(W/L)_{\text{pullup}}}{(W/L)_{\text{access}}} = \frac{360\,\text{nm}}{540\,\text{nm}} = 0.667 < 1.0
    \end{equation}
    Accounting for the nominal transconductance-parameter ratio $KP_p/KP_n = 100/250 = 0.40$, the pull-up-to-access strength-parameter ratio is approximately $0.267$. This sizing favors access-device conduction relative to the pull-up transistor during a write operation (with geometric Writability Ratio $\text{WR} = 1/\text{PR} = 1.50 > 1.0$). Actual writeability is verified through the simulated transient write-flip response.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Baselines at Nominal Corner ($27^\circ\text{C}, 1.0\,\text{V}$)}
Three distinct dynamic operations and two static DC characterizations are performed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hold Mode ($WL=0$, $BL=\overline{BL}=V_{DD}$):} The stored nominal row has $V(Q)=1.000$\,V, $V(Q_b)=0.000$\,V, and $I_{\mathrm{DDQ}}=4.2$\,nA. Current is sampled from the supply trace at 20\,ns; this alone does not demonstrate complete settling.
    \item \textbf{Read 1 / Read 0 Mode:} Bitlines are precharged to $V_{DD}$ with $C_{\mathrm{BL}}=20$\,fF. During Read 1, high $Q$ turns on pull-down $M_4$. Asserting $WL$ allows $\overline{BL}$ to discharge through $M_6$ and $M_4$ via $Q_b$. The stored nominal mean differential swing is 224.8\,mV at the fixed 2.15\,ns strobe. Sense delay is separately defined by the first 100\,mV differential crossing relative to the wordline's 50\% rise; its nominal mean is 60.0\,ps. The stored nominal maximum read bump is 125.0\,mV.
    \item \textbf{Write 1 / Write 0 Mode:} For Write 1, the drivers set $BL=V_{DD}$ and $\overline{BL}=0$. Asserting $WL$ turns on $M_6$, which pulls $Q_b$ down against pull-up $M_1$ and initiates regenerative flipping. The stored nominal mean delay across both write directions is 60.2\,ps.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Numerical Inscribed-Square SNM Butterfly Extraction}
\label{sec:snm_extract}
To measure Static Noise Margin, dedicated DC testbenches are executed where the feedback loop is opened in SPICE by duplicating the cell core. A DC voltage sweep is applied from $0\,\text{V}$ to $V_{DD}$ in $1.0\,\text{mV}$ increments to trace the forward and reverse Voltage Transfer Characteristics: $V(Q_b) = f(V_Q)$ and $V(Q) = g(V_{Q_b})$.

Our extraction engine evaluates the piecewise characteristics directly via numerical interpolation. For the upper-left lobe (Lobe 1), the maximum inscribed square side length $s_1$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
s_1 = \max_{t \in [0, V_{DD}], \, s \ge 0} s \quad \text{s.t.} \quad f(g(t) + s) \ge t + s
\label{eq:snm_lobe1}
\end{equation}
and dually for the lower-right lobe (Lobe 2):
\begin{equation}
s_2 = \max_{t \in [0, V_{DD}], \, s \ge 0} s \quad \text{s.t.} \quad g(f(t) + s) \ge t + s
\label{eq:snm_lobe2}
\end{equation}
yielding $\mathrm{SNM}=\min(s_1,s_2)$. The implementation samples 4,001 candidate positions and uses 50 bisection steps with piecewise-linear interpolation. Thus the continuous expressions describe the geometric objective, not an exact finite-grid solution for arbitrary curves. A zero result indicates that no positive lobe square was found under these assumptions; curve integrity must be assessed before assigning a physical failure mechanism.

Table~\ref{tab:snm_val} compares numerical results with rounded reference values recorded in the project's manual checks (470.00\,mV in Hold and 263.70\,mV in Read). These differences are consistency checks, not independent analytical error bounds or guarantees of sub-millivolt physical accuracy. Interpolation and the finite search grid limit resolution. Separate lobe evaluation permits asymmetric VTCs; monotonicity and curve-integrity assumptions must still be checked for faulty configurations.

\section{Parametric Fault Taxonomy \& Multi-Corner Simulation Matrix}
\label{sec:faults}

\subsection{Parametric Defect Taxonomy and Severity Tiers}
We implement a five-class defect taxonomy directly mapped to physical failure mechanisms in scaled CMOS memories via automated SPICE netlist mutation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Class 0 (Healthy Baseline):} The unmodified cell at each operating corner, denoted $S_0$. The healthy target and reference are run separately with the same deterministic circuit and settings.
    \item \textbf{Class 1 (Resistive Open Defect):} Models partial via/contact voids or metal electromigration in the access path. Injected as a series resistance $R_{\text{open}}$ on the bitline side of access pass-gate $M_5$ or $M_6$ across six severity tiers ($S_1$ to $S_6$):
    \begin{equation}
    R_{\text{open}} \in \{0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10\}\,\text{k}\Omega
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Class 2 (Bridging Defect):} Models inter-layer dielectric breakdown or metallic sliver shorts bridging the two internal complementary storage nodes. Injected as a parallel shunt resistor $R_{\text{bridge}}$ between nodes $Q$ and $Q_b$ across five severity tiers ($S_1$ to $S_5$):
    \begin{equation}
    R_{\text{bridge}} \in \{0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10\}\,\text{k}\Omega
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Class 3 ($V_{\text{th}}$ Storage Pair Drift):} Models NBTI/PBTI interface-state trapping and channel hot-carrier degradation in the cross-coupled inverters. Injected by shifting $|V_{\text{TO}}|$ symmetrically across inverter transistors $M_1$--$M_4$ across six tiers ($S_1$ to $S_6$, $+5\%$ to $+30\%$, representing $22.5\text{--}135\,\text{mV}$ shifts at TT, without a stress-time aging calibration):
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta V_{\text{th,store}} \in \{5,10,15,20,25,30\}\%
    \end{equation}
    For PMOS pull-up devices ($M_1, M_2$), a $+x\%$ shift denotes an increase in magnitude $|V_{\text{th}}|$, scaling $V_{\text{TO},p}$ more negative (from $-0.450\,\text{V}$ to $-0.585\,\text{V}$), thereby compressing drive current.
    \item \textbf{Class 4 ($V_{\text{th}}$ Access Pair Drift):} Models pass-gate dielectric charge trapping on access devices $M_5$ and $M_6$. Injected by shifting $V_{\text{TO},n}$ symmetrically on $M_5/M_6$ across six severity tiers ($S_1$ to $S_6$):
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta V_{\text{th,access}} \in \{5,10,15,20,25,30\}\%
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The severity tiers $S_1$ through $S_6$ map directly to discrete physical defect parameters: for Class 1, $S_1=0.2\,\text{k}\Omega$ to $S_6=10\,\text{k}\Omega$; for Class 2, $S_1=500\,\Omega$ to $S_5=10\,\text{k}\Omega$ (bridging terminates at $S_5$, so $S_6$ contains no bridging instances); and for Classes 3 and 4, $S_1=+5\%$ to $S_6=+30\%$.

\subsection{45-Corner Process-Voltage-Temperature (PVT) Operating Envelope \& Simulation Matrix}
To evaluate operational variations across the operating envelope, a 45-corner Process-Voltage-Temperature (PVT) matrix is constructed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Process Corners (3):} Typical-Typical (TT: nominal $V_{\text{TO},n} = 0.450\,\text{V}$, $KP_n = 250\,\mu\text{A}/\text{V}^2$, $V_{\text{TO},p} = -0.450\,\text{V}$, $KP_p = 100\,\mu\text{A}/\text{V}^2$), Fast-Fast (FF: $V_{\text{TO},n} = 0.405\,\text{V}$, $V_{\text{TO},p} = -0.405\,\text{V}$, $KP \times 1.10$), and Slow-Slow (SS: $V_{\text{TO},n} = 0.495\,\text{V}$, $V_{\text{TO},p} = -0.495\,\text{V}$, $KP \times 0.90$).
    \item \textbf{Supply Voltages ($V_{DD}$, 3):} $0.9\,\text{V}$ ($-10\%$), $1.0\,\text{V}$ (nominal), $1.1\,\text{V}$ ($+10\%$).
    \item \textbf{Temperatures ($T$, 5):} $-40^\circ\text{C}$ (industrial cold), $0^\circ\text{C}$, $27^\circ\text{C}$ (room nominal), $75^\circ\text{C}$, $125^\circ\text{C}$ (worst-case automotive thermal).
\end{itemize}
Combining 3 process corners $\times$ 15 Voltage-Temperature corners yields 45 distinct PVT corners without inter-device skew (FS/SF). Every corner contains 30 deterministic sample configurations (1 Healthy, 12 Resistive Open sweeps [6 on $M_5$, 6 on $M_6$], 5 Bridging sweeps, 6 Storage $V_{\text{th}}$ sweeps, and 6 Access $V_{\text{th}}$ sweeps), yielding:
\begin{equation}
N_{\text{total}} = 30 \times 45 = 1{,}350 \text{ simulated dataset rows}
\end{equation}
For each of the 1,350 instances, our automated multithreaded simulation runner executes 7 independent SPICE operational decks (Hold, Write 1, Write 0, Read 1, Read 0, SNM Hold DC, and SNM Read DC), totaling \textbf{9,450 SPICE runs (6,750 transient and 2,700 DC)}. In addition, for each of the 45 corners, a dedicated healthy reference simulation is executed under identical environmental and process parameters (315 additional runs, bringing total SPICE simulations to 9,765) to provide the per-corner normalization baseline. Waveforms are parsed for feature extraction; retained raw outputs are needed for independent measurement checks.

\subsection{Data Handling, Numerical Imputation, and Sensitivity to Solver Timeouts}
\label{sec:timeouts}
The dataset marks 1,327 of 1,350 rows as converged: 450/450 FF, 449/450 SS, and 428/450 TT. The other 23 rows are configuration--corner observations with one or more numerical or extraction failures, not necessarily 23 transient time-step failures. Nonmonotonic SNM traces may indicate distorted responses, but that warning alone does not establish physical loss of bistability.

The generator substitutes finite fallback values when extraction fails: unavailable write or sense delays may become 20,000\,ps (20\,ns), missing swings or SNM may become zero, and unavailable hold current may become $10I_{\mathrm{DDQ,ref}}$. These encode missing measurements or censoring and must not be interpreted as measured fault physics. Averaging over only available read/write directions may also mask a failed operation. A complete 34-column CSV with no NaNs is therefore not proof of complete valid measurements.

The primary LOCO comparison uses all 1,350 rows. For the converged-only comparison, we exclude the 23 rows marked nonconverged before forming the 45 folds, so both training and test sets use only the remaining 1,327 rows. On these same folds, the raw-feature RF achieves 75.28\% accuracy (999/1,327), 65.55\% balanced accuracy, 63.01\% macro-F1, and 43.33\% worst-corner accuracy. The RF with the normalized feature set achieves 99.70\% accuracy (1,323/1,327), 99.70\% balanced accuracy, and 99.76\% macro-F1, with 43 perfect corners and 90.00\% worst-corner accuracy. The stored convergence flag retains the healthy row with the rejected read-SNM sweep at TT, 0.9\,V, and $0^\circ$C (Section~\ref{sec:dispersion}). Section~\ref{sec:loco_results} reports the separate 44-corner check that excludes that corner from training and testing.

\section{Physics-Normalized Feature Engineering}
\label{sec:features}

\subsection{Extracted Dynamic and Static Features}
For every cell instance $i$ simulated at corner $c = (\text{Process}, V_{DD}, T)$, we extract dynamic transient metrics and static margin metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Timing Delays:} $t_{\text{write}}$ (time from $50\%$ $WL$ rise until internal node reaches $0.5 V_{DD}$) and $t_{\text{sense}}$ (time to develop $\Delta V_{\text{BL}}$).
    \item \textbf{Currents \& Voltages:} $I_{\text{DDQ}}$ (quiescent standby current), $I_{\text{write,peak}}$ (peak switching current), $\Delta V_{\text{BL}}$ (bitline strobe differential), and $V_{\text{bump}}$ (internal read disturbance).
    \item \textbf{Stability Margins:} $\text{SNM}_{\text{hold}}$, $\text{SNM}_{\text{read}}$, asymmetry $\Delta \text{SNM}$, and binary stability pass flags.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Formulation of Reference and Internal Ratios}
At each operating corner, a separate simulation of the healthy reference provides the denominator for target-cell measurements. These reference measurements are available at inference, including at the corner withheld from classifier training. The reference-normalized features are
\begin{align}
\text{iddq\_norm} &= \frac{I_{\text{DDQ,meas}}(c)}{I_{\text{DDQ,ref}}(c)} \\
\text{t\_write\_norm} &= \frac{t_{\text{write,meas}}(c)}{t_{\text{write,ref}}(c)} \\
\text{dv\_bl\_norm} &= \frac{\Delta V_{\text{BL,meas}}(c)}{\Delta V_{\text{BL,ref}}(c)} \\
\text{snm\_hold\_norm} &= \frac{\text{SNM}_{\text{hold,meas}}(c)}{\text{SNM}_{\text{hold,ref}}(c)} \\
\text{snm\_read\_norm} &= \frac{\text{SNM}_{\text{read,meas}}(c)}{\text{SNM}_{\text{read,ref}}(c)}
\end{align}
The feature set also includes three internal ratios.
\begin{align}
\text{read\_write\_delay\_ratio} &= \frac{t_{\text{sense}}}{t_{\text{write}}}, \\
\text{snm\_ratio} &= \frac{\text{SNM}_{\text{read}}}{\text{SNM}_{\text{hold}}} \\
\text{dv\_bl\_vdd\_ratio} &= \frac{\Delta V_{\text{BL}}}{V_{DD}}
\end{align}

The implemented reference denominators are floored at $10^{-4}\,\mu$A for current, 1\,ps for write delay, 1\,mV for swing, and 0.05\,V for each SNM. The internal delay and SNM ratios use denominator floors of 1\,ps and $10^{-4}$\,V. Rounding occurs at several extraction stages, so ratios reconstructed from the rounded CSV need not reproduce every stored normalized value. These regularizers and the fallback policy in Section~\ref{sec:timeouts} matter near zero responses.

\section{Experimental Results \& Cross-PVT Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocols}
In leave-one-corner-out (LOCO) evaluation, each fold trains on 44 corners (1,320 observations) and tests the remaining corner (30 observations). The 45 folds cover all sampled combinations of process setting, supply voltage, and temperature. Fault parameters and injection targets recur across the training and test corners. Sections~\ref{sec:loso} and~\ref{sec:joint} also evaluate held-out fault parameters.

\subsection{Evaluated Models}
We benchmark four classical machine learning classifiers spanning ensemble, decision-tree, and linear architectures, implemented using Scikit-learn \cite{pedregosa2011scikit}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Random Forest (RF):} 100 estimators, max depth 10, random state 42.
    \item \textbf{HistGradientBoosting (HistGB):} 100 iterations, max depth 6, random state 42.
    \item \textbf{Decision Tree (DT):} Max depth 8, random state 42.
    \item \textbf{Logistic Regression:} L2-regularized classifier, max 1,000 iterations, random state 42. The 45-corner benchmark script does not apply feature standardization; scaling and convergence effects limit architectural comparisons.
\end{itemize}

The full-data benchmark compares 12 raw features (five base measurements, peak write current, sense delay, read bump, and four status flags) with eight ratio features plus the same four flags. It is not a one-to-one normalization-only comparison; the matched five-feature ablation below controls that distinction. Accuracy is pooled across held-out rows. Balanced accuracy is mean class recall, and macro-F1 is mean per-class F1. Class counts are 45 healthy, 540 open, 225 bridge, and 270 in each threshold-shift class. Equal 30-row corner sizes make pooled accuracy equal the mean of fold accuracies. Population standard deviations ($\mathrm{ddof}=0$) across the 45 folds describe variation across correlated operating conditions, not confidence intervals over independent devices. No nested tuning or independent-device replication is claimed.

\subsection{Cross-PVT LOCO Diagnostic Results}
\label{sec:loco_results}
Table~\ref{tab:loco_master} and Fig.~\ref{fig:loco_bars} summarize the 45-corner LOCO results.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Raw-feature performance.} RF mean LOCO accuracy is 75.11\%, with a 50.00\% floor at TT and SS, 0.9\,V, $-40^\circ$C. Similar delay changes for access-path resistance and threshold shifts can contribute to confusion between those classes.
    \item \textbf{Reference-normalized performance.} RF accuracy reaches 99.33\% (1,341/1,350), a gain of 24.22 percentage points, and its worst-corner accuracy rises from 50.00\% to 90.00\%. HistGB reaches 99.41\% (1,342/1,350), a gain of 21.85 points, and its worst-corner accuracy rises from 40.00\% to 86.67\%. RF and HistGB classify every observation correctly at 39 and 40 corners, respectively.
    \item \textbf{Classifier Comparison:} Decision Tree accuracy rises from 73.56\% to 98.44\%, whereas the implemented Logistic Regression rises from 80.59\% to 86.89\%. These are results for the stated configurations. The unstandardized linear model is not a sufficient basis for claiming that nonlinear architectures are intrinsically required.
\end{enumerate}

To check sensitivity to the flagged read sweeps in Section~\ref{sec:dispersion}, we started with all 1,350 rows and omitted the 30 observations at TT, 0.9\,V, and $0^\circ$C before forming the LOCO folds. The normalized 12-feature RF achieved 99.32\% (1,311/1,320) across the remaining 44 corners. In a separate 45-corner check, removing the normalized read SNM, read-to-hold SNM ratio, and combined bistability flag gave 99.56\% (1,344/1,350) with the same RF settings.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports 99.48\% accuracy, 99.15\% balanced accuracy, and 99.20\% macro-F1 for the eight-ratio model. Figure~\ref{fig:confusion_matrix}, by contrast, is the full 12-feature RF model, whose class recalls are:

The false positive occurs at TT, $0^\circ$C, 1.0\,V, in a row flagged as nonconverged.
    \item \textbf{Class 1 (Resistive Opens):} $99.63\%$ (538 of 540 correct; 1 misattributed as healthy and 1 as storage $V_{\text{th}}$ drift).

\subsection{Sensitivity to Row-Wise Feature Noise}
\label{sec:tolerance}
The feature-perturbation experiment applies a row-wise multiplicative perturbation to all eight ratio features:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde z_{ij}=z_{ij}(1+\epsilon_i),\qquad
\epsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2).
\end{equation}
The same scalar $\epsilon_i$ is shared across features of row $i$, including three internal ratios that do not use a healthy reference. Gaussian draws use seed 42, with $\sigma$ from 0 to 0.15. Both training and test rows are perturbed before LOCO fitting, and a classifier is retrained for each noise level.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{fig6_reference_mismatch_sensitivity.png}

\subsection{Joint PVT and Defect Parameter Holdout}
\label{sec:joint}
Each fold withholds one operating corner and excludes ranks $S_3$ and $S_4$ from the other 44 corners. Training uses the healthy tier $S_0$ and the available fault ranks $S_1$, $S_2$, $S_5$, and $S_6$. Testing uses only faulty observations at $S_3$ and $S_4$ from the withheld corner. The RF using eight ratio features correctly classifies 446 of 450 observations (99.11\% accuracy and 99.03\% balanced accuracy). This test evaluates interpolation at withheld fault-parameter levels and an operating-corner combination absent from training.

\section{Stated Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
The benchmark uses generic Level-1 device cards and heuristic global process shifts. The 30 configurations recur across the 45 operating corners, with symmetric threshold shifts and one defect at a time. The study covers variation across the sampled grid. Calibrated process models, independent device mismatch, asymmetric aging, extracted parasitics, and silicon measurements would extend that scope. In particular, sampled standby-current trajectories are nonmonotonic at several fixed-voltage settings, so a leakage-law interpretation would require settled-current and solver-tolerance checks.

Classification assumes access to a matched healthy reference at each test corner. Healthy recall characterizes deterministic matches. An independent healthy population would be needed to estimate false alarms under mismatch. The feature-noise experiment measures refitted models, and deployment-only reference errors remain untested. Full-data classification can use information carried by fallback-coded delay, current, and SNM values. The converged-only result measures sensitivity to excluding rows marked as nonconverged.

Internal-node SNM sweeps and picosecond timing fit characterization structures with analog access. Applying the method to a production array would require a reference-health check and measurement circuitry with characterized precision, routing, area, and test time. The screening checks used here cover the simulated operations. March-sequence coverage would require a separate study.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Reference normalization improves five-class SRAM fault diagnosis over the 45-corner simulation grid. RF accuracy increases from 75.11\% to 99.33\%, and its worst-corner accuracy rises from 50.00\% to 90.00\%. On rows marked converged, RF accuracy is 75.28\% with raw features and 99.70\% with the normalized feature set. The 5\,k$\Omega$ open comparisons show lower cross-corner variation in write delay and bitline swing at both injection sites, with residual read-SNM variation.

The joint corner and parameter holdout yields 99.11\% accuracy, while accuracy on the weakest tested opens falls to 76.67\% in the $S_1$ parameter holdout. Testing independent healthy and faulty device realizations with a calibrated model would establish how these results carry over to a physical reference measurement.
